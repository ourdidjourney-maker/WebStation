                                      Start here

                                      Double-click the file. That is the installation.

                                      There is no setup, no build step, no package manager and no first-run wizard. WebStation is one HTML document. Open it in any modern browser and the whole workspace is running: five panels, thirty-two applications, and every piece of your work stored on your own machine.

                                      Nothing to install

                                      No runtime, no framework, no CDN. Every line of it was written for this project.

                                      Nothing to sign into

                                      No account, no email, no token, no cookie. The file has no idea who you are.

                                      Nothing to lose

                                      Your work is saved in your browser and exported with one button. No lock-in, no service to pay.

                                      Take it with you. Put the file on a USB stick and it runs on any machine you plug it into. Your data stays in whichever browser you opened it in, and Backup writes everything — documents, images, recordings and all — into a single JSON file you can carry alongside it.

                                      Features

                                      The systems that make this workspace what it is

                                      The flip

                                      Switching apps is a physical turn, not a tab change

                                      1

                                      The card rotates out, the two apps swap at the midpoint, and it rotates back in. The incoming app is never visible until the card has turned past its own edge, so you never see a half-drawn screen.

                                      2

                                      Flipping right turns one way and flipping left turns the other, which means the deck has a real spatial order. After a day you stop reading the names and just know that Trash is two to the right of Paint.

                                      3

                                      Every face behind the card stays alive while it is hidden. A timer keeps counting, a recording keeps recording, a video keeps playing, a garden keeps growing.

                                      4

                                      Hover any app name and that app appears live in place, framed in blue, without committing to the flip. A short delay stops it flickering as you move along the row.

                                      5

                                      This is what made the whole project possible. Five panels holding a deck of apps each is thirty-two apps on one screen with no window management, no taskbar, no overlapping windows and nothing to arrange.

                                      The peeked app is given its own solid backing, so two transparent apps can never show through each other.

                                      Hover peek

                                      See an app without opening it

                                      1

                                      Hover any app name in a switcher and that app appears, live, in place, framed in blue. Not a preview, not a thumbnail, not a tooltip — the running app, in the panel, with its real contents.

                                      2

                                      Move your pointer away and it is gone. Nothing was opened, nothing was closed, and whatever you were doing is still exactly where you left it.

                                      3

                                      This is the other half of what made a deck of apps on one screen possible. The flip gave them somewhere to live; peek meant you never have to leave what you are doing to check something.

                                      4

                                      You can run your eye along a switcher and watch five apps go past in a second. Is there anything in the bin? Did that recording save? What was on that sticky note? Answered without a single click.

                                      5

                                      A 180 millisecond delay stops it flickering as you move along a row, so brushing past a switcher on the way somewhere else never sets it off.

                                      6

                                      The peeked app gets its own solid frosted backing, so two transparent apps can never show through each other and leave you reading a double image.

                                      Combined with the flip, this is why the workspace has no taskbar, no window list, no minimise and nothing to arrange. You look at what you want to look at.

                                      Safe Delete

                                      A bin that gives you the time back, not just the file

                                      1

                                      Every other trash can is a holding pen: things sit there until you empty it. This one lets you set how long something has left, per item: one minute, thirty minutes, an hour, twelve, twenty-four, forty-eight.

                                      2

                                      With a time set, pressing × does not delete. The item greys out and a live countdown starts, ticking in hours, minutes and seconds. The × becomes ↻ so you can call it back at any moment.

                                      3

                                      Twenty seconds before anything goes for good, the workspace tells you. The entire interface blurs and dims, and the notice is the only sharp thing on screen.

                                      4

                                      You get Stop, Go to bin or Continue. Go to bin sharpens just the Trash panel and highlights that exact item, leaving everything else soft.

                                      5

                                      Stop halts the clock and leaves the Trash button pulsing until you have actually looked at it. The workspace refuses to quietly forget that something is waiting on you.

                                      6

                                      Pending deletions also appear in the Task Manager under Running now, with their countdown, because a deletion in progress is something the machine is doing.

                                      Nothing in this workspace is ever destroyed without a warning, a countdown, and a way back.

                                      Catching deleted text

                                      The only workspace that keeps the words you backspaced out

                                      1

                                      A browser keeps no record of a deleted character. Once it is gone it is gone, which is why no editor offers this.

                                      2

                                      So the keystroke is intercepted before the deletion happens: a one-character range is built behind or in front of the cursor and read while the character still exists.

                                      3

                                      Holding backspace would then produce hundreds of single-letter entries, which is useless. Deletions within 900 milliseconds of each other are merged into one.

                                      4

                                      Each character is prepended to the front, because backspace eats backwards. That rebuilds the passage in its original order rather than reversed.

                                      5

                                      So holding backspace through a sentence gives you one readable entry: “quick brown fox jumps (20 characters)”. Pause longer than 900ms and the next deletion starts a fresh entry.

                                      6

                                      Put back reinserts the text into your document at your cursor. Hover any entry to read the whole passage without opening it.

                                      The same bin catches cleared spreadsheet cells as one restorable block, sticky notes, tasks, vault entries, bookmarks, slides, gallery images and clipboard history.

                                      Weather, per panel

                                      Every panel has its own sky

                                      1

                                      Rain, snow, wind, lightning and the seasons are tracked separately for every panel. One can be in a thunderstorm while the panel beside it sits in calm sunlight.

                                      2

                                      Rain falls in three depth layers for parallax, each streak a soft gradient rather than a hard line. Each shower rolls its own weight: barely spitting, steady, heavy, or a downpour. Light rain uses thinner, shorter, fainter streaks rather than simply fewer of them.

                                      3

                                      Water behaves like water. Beads cling to the glass, heavier ones release sooner, they stretch as they accelerate, wobble off course, and leave a wet trail that thins and dries behind them.

                                      4

                                      Lightning carries in fewer than half of storms. Three multi-stroke flicker patterns, brightness banded into four distances, and the light falls directionally using screen blending, so it lights the flowers, the rain and the leaves together rather than washing the screen white.

                                      5

                                      Wind is owned by the panel, so leaves never cross in opposite directions, and it only changes direction when the air is clear. Leaves travel at a capped speed rather than a capped time, so none of them crawl in a narrow panel.

                                      6

                                      Seasons scale to the garden. A season runs 1.6 to 2.8 times the growing time you chose, so a plant is never cut down before it has had a chance to bloom.

                                      Storms arrive and leave rather than switching. Snow drifts in over nine seconds; when it passes, the wind drops first, the flakes already falling finish their fall, and the drift melts rather than vanishing.

                                      The heat lamp

                                      An interface that needs snow clearing

                                      1

                                      When winter comes to a panel, snow settles along the bottom of it and piles up, with an uneven drifted top edge that deepens as the storm runs.

                                      2

                                      A ☀ then appears in that panel’s toolbar — and only while there is snow to melt. The control exists when it is meaningful and vanishes when it is not.

                                      3

                                      It strikes like a real bulb. Press it and it flashes in spurts, catches, drops out for a moment, then comes up to temperature slowly over seven seconds.

                                      4

                                      It fights the weather rather than cancelling it. The snow keeps falling. What changes is that it can no longer settle: the drift sinks from the top down and runs off as meltwater beads down the glass.

                                      5

                                      Water dries faster on hot glass. While the lamp is on, beads run and evaporate in about a third of the usual time. Switch it off and the glass stays warm for a while, drying slower, before returning to normal.

                                      6

                                      Switching off is a cooling, not a switch. The warmth falls away over eight seconds. And like any real lamp on a thermostat, it gives up on its own after a few minutes.

                                      A user interface that occasionally requires snow clearing is, as far as anyone can tell, a first.

                                      Proximity and blur

                                      The workspace decides what deserves your attention

                                      1

                                      Panels you are not using blur, desaturate, dim and fade. The one you approach sharpens as your pointer nears it. All four properties animate together from a single ratio with an eased falloff.

                                      2

                                      Reach is measured from the panel’s shorter side, not its diagonal, so a full-width panel does not clear from across the screen.

                                      3

                                      This is why a screen this dense is not exhausting. Most software assumes everything should be equally sharp at all times, which gives your eye no permission to ignore anything.

                                      4

                                      Two hard problems had to be solved for it. Embedded pages swallow every mouse event, so a transparent tracking layer sits over blurred panels. And CSS filters apply to every descendant, so the unblur control had to live outside the element it unblurs.

                                      5

                                      The garden blurs and wakes in lockstep with its panel, so the weather never sharpens against a soft panel.

                                      The same idea does the heavy lifting elsewhere: the whole workspace steps back behind a pending deletion, a finished task timer, or a network warning, leaving one sharp thing to answer.

                                      The network seal

                                      An OS that tells you the moment it can be overheard

                                      1

                                      The control group carries a live state: Sealed when nothing is leaving your machine, and Network open the moment anything reaches out. Hover it and it names exactly what is connected.

                                      2

                                      Before anything connects, a warning drops from the top of the screen and the workspace blurs behind it. It names what is about to happen and what it exposes: your IP address, your rough location, your device and browser, the time you asked and what you asked for.

                                      3

                                      Nothing loads until you answer. Cancel stops it dead, and the workspace stays sealed.

                                      4

                                      Three things can reach out: the browser panel, the news ticker, and a live link you open yourself in Post. Everything else — every app, document and picture — has nowhere to go and no code to get there.

                                      5

                                      A feed that is switched on is reported as an open route for as long as it is on, not only during the seconds it happens to be fetching. The honest statement is that a way out exists.

                                      6

                                      One thing it cannot see: links you click inside a page in the browser panel. That page belongs to another site and the browser forbids reading where it went, so the monitor says you followed a link from wherever you started rather than claiming to know.

                                      5

                                      RSS feed switches the ticker off outright: every request in flight is aborted mid-flight, new ones are refused at the source, and the cache is cleared. Verified at zero outside requests.

                                      6

                                      Scanned with an independent privacy analyser, the workspace reports 0 external connections, 0 trackers, 0 corporate owners, and an exposure score of 0 out of 100.

                                      7

                                      Three things can reach out, and each asks first: the browser panel, the news ticker, and a live link you open yourself in Post. A live link goes straight to the other machine — there is no server in it, which is why it is the only kind of messaging here.

                                      Compare that with an ordinary news website, which typically pulls in thirty or more scripts from a dozen companies before you have read a word.

                                      Sealed by construction

                                      Not a setting. There is nothing there to switch on.

                                      1

                                      No server. Not a slow one, not a private one. There is nothing to breach, nothing to leak, nothing to subpoena and nothing to go down.

                                      2

                                      No accounts. No sign-up, no email, no session, no cookie, no fingerprint, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting. The file has no idea who you are and no way to find out.

                                      3

                                      No third-party code. Not one line. Which means zero supply-chain risk: no dependency can be compromised upstream, because there are no dependencies.

                                      4

                                      No code execution. eval and new Function appear zero times in the whole document. Nothing you paste, import or restore can become running code.

                                      5

                                      Your files stay yours. The disk browser reads names and sizes only, and there is no upload path in the code because there is nowhere to upload to.

                                      6

                                      You can audit it yourself. It is one readable text file with no minification and no obfuscation. Open it in any editor and search it. Try that with a native application or a cloud service.

                                      Backup writes everything you own into a single readable file you keep. No export fee, no lock-in, no proprietary format, no service to keep paying. If this project vanished tomorrow, your copy would keep working.

                                      Wear

                                      Buttons that age where your thumb actually lands

                                      1

                                      Talk to a pilot and they can tell you which switches get used without reading a label, because the panel tells them. Thousands of presses polish the finish, rub the legend away, and open the paint to bare metal.

                                      2

                                      Every button’s face is divided into a 9 × 5 grid, and each press deposits wear into the exact cell the pointer landed in, with a little bleeding into its neighbours.

                                      3

                                      Wear accumulates, it never moves. Hit the left edge and later the right edge and you get two distinct worn patches, exactly as you would on metal.

                                      4

                                      It ages in the order real surfaces fail: the finish polishes first, then the printed legend rubs off, then the paint thins, then bare alloy shows with a dark rim where the paint lifted.

                                      5

                                      The curve is logarithmic and slow on purpose. A button you press twenty times a day starts to shine in about a week, shows obvious polish in a month, and reaches metal at about four months.

                                      6

                                      So after a month of real use, your workspace shows you where your hands live. It is the most honest telemetry there is, because it never leaves your machine and it is for you.

                                      The legend never wears past readable — that is a hard limit. And Refinish restores the whole panel to new, like sending it out for respray.

                                      Hot metal text

                                      Words that arrive at temperature

                                      1

                                      Type with Effects on and each character lands in its normal colour first, so your eye reads the letter before the effect starts. Then it heats through orange into blazing yellow and cools back to white.

                                      2

                                      Fast typing leaves a glowing wake behind the cursor with every letter at a different temperature.

                                      3

                                      And the letters shed slag. Molten beads run off and fall down the panel under gravity, cooling from white-yellow through gold and orange to dull red as they drift and die out.

                                      4

                                      The slag is deliberately uneven. Some letters barely drip, a few really run, and about one in five holds a bead and lets it go up to two seconds later — so drips keep arriving from letters you typed a moment ago.

                                      5

                                      Deleted letters burn up. Backspace throws the character off the screen, igniting orange and burning through yellow to white as it tumbles.

                                      6

                                      Sticky notes get the same treatment: deleting one peels it off the board from its top-left corner like tape letting go, then tumbles it away with its own spin and distance.

                                      This was refined by an animator who said the first version was not turbulent enough, and she was right: every letter was dripping in lockstep, which read as a wave.

                                      1

                                      2

                                      3

                                      4

                                      Storage that degrades instead of failing

                                      It will not lose your work quietly

                                      1

                                      Everything is saved through a single layer with three tiers. IndexedDB is the default, with a large quota and room for images, PDFs and recordings.

                                      2

                                      If IndexedDB is blocked — private browsing, a locked-down machine, an odd browser — it falls back to localStorage automatically. No error, no lost work.

                                      3

                                      If even that is unavailable it runs from memory, so the workspace still works. And the Task Manager says so in plain language: data is not being saved.

                                      4

                                      That last part matters. Most software either pretends everything is fine or refuses to start. This tells you the truth and keeps working.

                                      5

                                      Everything sits behind an in-memory cache, so all thirty-two apps read instantly after boot with no waiting on storage.

                                      6

                                      Backup writes every key and every binary file into one dated file. Restore reads it back inside a single transaction that rolls back completely if anything fails, so you can never end up half-restored.

                                      6

                                      Zoom that knows what you meant

                                      The wheel does the right thing without being told

                                      1

                                      Roll the wheel over a panel and it zooms from 50% to 200%. But it only zooms when there is nothing genuinely scrollable under your pointer, so long lists, the spreadsheet and text areas still scroll normally.

                                      2

                                      3

                                      4

                                      The screensaver

                                      The interface leaves, the world stays

                                      1

                                      After a while with no input, the entire interface fades away over two seconds — every panel, the ticker, the status bar. What is left is the glass and the garden.

                                      2

                                      The garden then runs fuller than it ever does while you are working: every panel gets planted, more flowers at once, and the seasons keep turning.

                                      3

                                      Any movement or keypress brings it all back, just as slowly. Nothing snaps.

                                      4

                                      You choose the wait: 1, 5, 20, 30 or 60 minutes, or off entirely. Off means no timer is scheduled at all.

                                      It is the only screensaver that is showing you something the machine was already doing, rather than a separate program pretending to be busy.

                                      Every sound is generated

                                      There are no audio files in this workspace

                                      1

                                      The timer chime, the alarm, the checkers pieces, the crowning fanfare, the win and lose motifs — none of them are recordings.

                                      2

                                      They are synthesised on the spot with Web Audio: an oscillator, an envelope, a frequency. A few lines each.

                                      3

                                      Which is why a workspace with this much in it is still small enough to be startling. Sound files are heavy; the instructions to make a sound are almost nothing.

                                      4

                                      It also means they cannot go missing, cannot fail to load, and work with the network unplugged.

                                      Messages with no provider

                                      An address nobody issued and nobody can take away

                                      1

                                      On first run the workspace generates two keypairs with the browser’s own cryptography: ECDH P-256 for sealing and ECDSA P-256 for signing. The private halves never leave the machine.

                                      2

                                      Your address is the public halves. There is no registration, no directory, no provider and no recovery path — because a recovery path would be a third party.

                                      3

                                      A message is encrypted to the recipient with a key derived from both parties, then signed. The signature covers the ciphertext, so tampering is caught before anything is decrypted.

                                      4

                                      What comes out is a block of text between BEGIN and END markers. That block is the message. It does not care how it travels — a chat, an SMS, a memory stick, a sheet of paper.

                                      5

                                      Which means it cannot be blocked, cannot be deplatformed, and works with the network unplugged. A human carrying it is a valid transport.

                                      6

                                      Verified: a single altered character is rejected, and the plaintext never appears in the armour.

                                      The trade stated plainly: the keys are long-lived, so there is no forward secrecy in this version. That is a known limit with a known fix, not an oversight.

                                      A link with no introducer

                                      WebRTC without a signalling server

                                      1

                                      WebRTC connects two browsers directly, but normally a company has to introduce them. That introduction is the part everyone assumes requires a server.

                                      2

                                      Here the human is the introduction. One machine produces a code, a person carries it across by any means, the other pastes it and sends one back. After that the two machines talk directly.

                                      3

                                      The reason a manual swap is enough: the workspace waits for candidate gathering to finish before serialising the description, so everything needed is inside that one block. There is no second channel trickling information.

                                      4

                                      Once open it is a direct encrypted pipe with nobody in the middle — nothing to subpoena, nothing to log, nothing to switch off.

                                      5

                                      One pipe carries several kinds of traffic, told apart by a prefix: live chat, sealed envelopes, and pings. Apps ride it without owning it.

                                      6

                                      The honest limit: the codes are valid for one session only, because they contain that session’s addresses and fingerprint. Each session needs one fresh swap.

                                      Night

                                      3

                                      4

                                      Each lamp has its own colour and its own brightness, and one value drives the glass, the light and the insects together so they can never disagree.

                                      5

                                      6

                                      Parental mode

                                      One switch, and nothing can be destroyed

                                      1

                                      A single button in the control group. While it is on, nothing in the workspace can be deleted.

                                      2

                                      Architecture

                                      Five panels, each holding a deck of applications

                                      The workspace is five panels. Each panel holds a card, and each card holds several faces. A face is a complete application. Only one face is visible per panel at a time — but every face stays alive in the background. A timer keeps counting, a recording keeps recording, a video keeps playing and a garden keeps growing, whichever face happens to be showing.

                                      Hover a panel to see its deck. This is the same layout the workspace boots with — and every one of these apps can be moved to any other panel in the Layout editor.

                                      Why it is built this way

                                      Three decisions do most of the work, and they are the reason a brand new application can be added in minutes rather than days:

                                      • A face is a black box. It owns its own markup, styling, state and storage key, and never reaches into another app. Nothing is shared, so nothing can collide.
                                      • The systems discover, they are not told. Flip, zoom, pan, blur, the garden, the screensaver and the proximity focus all work from structure — .panel and .face — not from a list of application names. A new app inherits every one of them at birth.
                                      • One storage interface, one deletion interface. Which means new apps appear in the Files browser, in Backup, in Restore, in the byte counts and in the Trash bin automatically.

                                      The proof: the checkers engine was lifted out of a 22,000-line application and became a fully integrated WebStation app — saved, resizable, in the Task Manager and the Layout editor, blurring with the weather and growing a garden over it — in a single pass.

                                      The flip system

                                      Switching apps is a physical card turn

                                      Applications do not appear and disappear. The card rotates out, the faces swap at the midpoint, and the card rotates back in — so the incoming app is never visible until the card has turned past its own edge. The direction follows the order of the deck, which means the arrangement is spatial and you build a memory of where things are.

                                      Glass runs a 710 ms turn with motion blur and a specular sheen sweeping across the face; Performance mode runs a clean 500 ms with no filters.

                                      Hover peek

                                      Hover any application button and that application appears live in place, framed in blue, without committing to the flip. A 180 ms delay stops it flickering as you move across the row, and the peeked face is given its own solid frosted backing so two transparent apps never show through each other.

                                      Storage

                                      Three tiers, with automatic fallback

                                      Everything you do is saved locally through a single storage layer that degrades gracefully rather than failing:

                                      TierBehaviour
                                      IndexedDBThe default. Large quota, holds binary files — images, PDFs, recordings and your chosen folder handle.
                                      localStorageAutomatic fallback if IndexedDB is blocked or unavailable.
                                      MemoryFinal fallback so the workspace still runs, with the Task Manager stating plainly that data is not being saved.

                                      All of it sits behind an in-memory cache, so every application reads synchronously after boot with no waiting. 21 storage keys hold the workspace, plus a separate binary store.

                                      Backup writes every key and every binary file, base64-encoded, into one dated JSON file. Restore reads it back inside a single transaction that rolls back completely if anything fails — so a bad restore can never leave you half-migrated.

                                      Trash & Safe Delete

                                      Nothing disappears without your consent

                                      Everything deleted goes to the bin first — vault entries, sticky notes, tasks, cleared spreadsheet cells, bookmarks, slides, gallery images, and every character deleted from the word processor.

                                      Capturing deleted text

                                      The browser keeps no record of a deleted character, so the keystroke is intercepted before the deletion happens: a one-character range is built behind or in front of the cursor and read. Holding backspace would then produce hundreds of single-letter entries, so deletions within 900 milliseconds of each other merge into one, each character prepended to the front — because backspace eats backwards — which rebuilds the passage in its original order.

paused, then deleted again
  →  a second, separate entry

                                      Hovering an entry shows the whole passage in a themed panel that follows your cursor.

                                      Safe Delete

                                      Each item has its own button that cycles through a hold time:

                                      Safe delete → 1 min → 30 min → 1 hour → 12 hours → 24 hours → 48 hours → Safe delete

                                      With a time set, pressing × does not delete. The item greys out and starts a live countdown in hours, minutes and seconds. The × becomes a ↺ so you can call it back at any moment, and when the clock reaches zero it is permanently flushed.

                                      The warning

                                      Twenty seconds before a permanent deletion, a notice rises gently into the corner — wherever you are and whatever you are doing. The entire workspace blurs behind it: every panel, the garden, the weather and the ticker all soften and dim, leaving the notice as the only sharp thing on screen. Nothing is blocked and nothing is frozen.

                                      The living biome

                                      A garden grows inside the panels you work in

                                      This is not decoration laid over the top. The plants grow inside the panels, behind your documents and spreadsheets, and they are drawn as live vector art — no images, no sprites.

                                      Daisy · round · tulip · petal · star · bell · poppy · cosmos · rose · sunflower · bud — every one assembled from paths at run time.

                                      Vector correctness

                                      This was the hard part, and most of the bugs in the project lived here. Flower heads ride the stem using motion along the actual stem path. Leaves are positioned by evaluating the stem's cubic bezier rather than interpolating in a straight line, and they scale from their join point rather than from a bounding box. Leaves are assigned evenly spaced slots along the stem so they can never stack on top of each other.

                                      Measured worst gap between a leaf and its stem across 54 leaves: 0.5 SVG units.

                                      Growth is unpredictable by design

                                      Each plant takes between one and sixty minutes depending on the range you choose, and every gap between sprouts rolls its own character rather than using a fixed interval:

                                      ChanceGap
                                      22%one right after another
                                      33%fairly brisk
                                      27%taking its time
                                      13%a long quiet stretch
                                      5%nothing for ages

                                      Every chosen gap is then multiplied by a further random factor, and 18% of the time a cluster of two to four plants comes up together. The result has no rhythm you can spot.

                                      Clipping

                                      Click any painted part of a plant and it is cut. Growth freezes at the instant of the cut, the plant springs up off the stem, turns over at the top of its arc and falls away — spinning anywhere from 120° to 2,200°. A new plant comes up elsewhere in that panel some seconds later.

                                      Weather

                                      Every panel has its own sky

                                      Rain, snow, wind and lightning are tracked per panel. One panel can be in a thunderstorm while the panel beside it sits in calm sunlight.

                                      Rain

                                      Three depth layers give parallax — near drops long, fast and bright, far ones hairline and faint. Every streak is a soft gradient rather than a hard line, which is what reads as motion blur. Each shower rolls its own weight: 30% barely spitting, 42% steady, 21% heavy, 7% a downpour — and light rain uses thinner, shorter, fainter streaks rather than simply fewer of them.

                                      Water on the glass

                                      Beads are built from three layered gradients — a bright highlight top-left, a secondary bounce bottom-right, and a cool refracted body — and they blur and brighten what is behind them. They cling for a random stretch, heavier ones releasing sooner, then stretch as they accelerate, wobble off course and leave a wet trail that thins and dries.

                                      Lightning

                                      Only 45% of storms carry it. Three multi-stroke flicker patterns — one bright stroke with a weaker return, a dim leader before the main strike, and a stuttering multi-stroke — with brightness banded into four distances from a faint horizon glow to a blinding overhead hit. The light falls directionally and uses screen blending, so it lights the flowers, the rain and the leaves together rather than washing the screen white.

                                      Wind

                                      Each panel runs two or three independent gust streams plus a constant background trickle, so bursts overlap and never form a repeating rhythm. The panel owns the wind direction, so leaves never cross in opposite directions, and the wind only turns around when the air is clear. Leaves travel at a capped speed rather than a capped time, so no leaf ever crawls regardless of panel width.

                                      The seasons

                                      Every panel with a garden turns through the year, and the season scales with the grow time you choose — 1.6 to 2.8 times the grow range — so a plant is never cut down before it has had time to bloom. Each panel rolls its own, so they never march in step.

                                      Wear

                                      The workspace remembers your hands

                                      Talk to a fighter pilot and they can tell you which switches get used without reading a label, because the panel tells them. Thousands of thumb presses polish the finish, rub the legend away, thin the paint and open it to bare aluminium. The switches nobody touches stay factory fresh for decades.

                                      WebStation does the same thing. After a month of real use, your workspace shows you where your hands live — and it is the most honest telemetry there is, because it never leaves your machine and it is for you.

                                      Accelerated heavily so you can see it in a few clicks. Press one spot repeatedly, then a different spot, and watch the patches accumulate separately.

                                      It is a map, not a spot

                                      Each button's face is divided into a 9 × 5 grid, and every press deposits wear into the exact cell the pointer landed in, with roughly a quarter bleeding into the neighbouring cells. This is the detail that makes it work. The first version tracked a single averaged contact point, which meant new presses moved the smudge. Real wear never moves — it accumulates. Hit the left edge and later the right edge, and you get two distinct worn patches, exactly as you would on metal.

                                      It ages in the right order

                                      StageWhat happens
                                      PolishThe finish goes glossy at the contact point. The button gets shinier before it gets damaged.
                                      LegendThe silkscreened label begins to rub away — always the weakest layer on a real button.
                                      PaintThe anodising thins and the substrate starts to show through.
                                      MetalDull scuffed alloy, with a dark rim where the paint has lifted and a faint brushed grain across it.

                                      Each patch gets a steady jitter on its radius and offset derived from its own grid position, so no two are the same shape and none are perfect circles — a circle reads as a graphic, an irregular blotch reads as damage.

                                      The curve

                                      Depth is logarithmic. The first hundred presses barely register, the interesting range is in the thousands, and it flattens out so nothing ever becomes unusable.

                                         ~30 presses    nothing visible
  ~150 presses    the finish starts to shine
  ~400 presses    a clear polished patch
~1,000 presses    the paint begins to open
~1,800 presses    bare alloy showing
~2,600 presses    fully worn through at that spot

                                      In real terms, a button you press twenty times a day starts to shine in about a week, shows obvious polish in a month, and reaches bare metal at roughly four months.

                                      Legibility is sacred. The label is lifted into its own layer that sits above every wear effect, so it can never be washed out or blended. Refinish restores the whole panel to new — like sending it out for respray. Wear is off by default, and unavailable in Performance mode.

                                      Writing effects

                                      Letters arrive hot and burn up when deleted

                                      The strike

                                      Each character lands in its normal colour first — so your eye reads the letter before the effect starts — then heats through orange into blazing yellow and cools back to white over 900 to 1,400 milliseconds. Fast typing leaves a glowing wake behind the cursor with every letter at a different temperature.

                                      Slag

                                      Every letter sheds two to four molten beads that run off it and fall under gravity, cooling from white-yellow through gold and orange to dull red as they drift sideways and die out.

                                      Deletion

                                      Backspace throws the character off the screen — up, over and away — igniting orange and burning through yellow to white as it tumbles, spinning anywhere from 90° to 2,000°.

                                      Sticky notes peel

                                      Deleting a note curls it away from its top-left corner like tape letting go, then tumbles it off the board with its own direction, spin and distance.

                                      All of it sits behind an Effects button in the word processor toolbar.
